About

Energy storage that moves with you.

I am Mohd Saqib, a research scholar working on rechargeable batteries beyond conventional lithium-ion. My work spans lithium, sodium and, above all, zinc-ion batteries.

Zinc is abundant, inexpensive and works with water-based electrolytes, which makes zinc-ion cells inherently safer than flammable organic systems. That safety matters most when a battery sits against the skin. My research focuses on flexible, wearable zinc-ion batteries: cells that keep working while they are bent, folded or twisted.

I also study how zinc is stored in cathodes, using operando Raman and ToF-SIMS to follow the interphase as the cell works. I design polymer and gel electrolytes that replace leak-prone liquids, stabilise the zinc anode against dendrites and side reactions, and stay mechanically robust under deformation. I then put them into full flexible devices and test them.

I came to batteries from electronics: my earlier work covered VLSI circuit design (VCOs, SRAM cells) and flexible piezoelectric sensors. That background shapes how I think about batteries as part of complete wearable systems.
